Topic 10

Monitoring Heartrate

01

Monitoring Heartrate

Know your rhythm.
Move with care.

Heart-rate monitoring helps you understand how hard your body is working during exercise.

♥
02

Why it matters

Find your exercise intensity.

Your heart rate gives you feedback while you exercise. It can help you build endurance without pushing beyond your current fitness level.

  • Resting heart rate shows your starting point.
  • Exercise heart rate shows your current effort.
  • Recovery heart rate shows how quickly you settle down.
03

How to check

Three simple ways

01Use a fitness watch or heart-rate monitor.
02Count your pulse at your wrist or neck for 15 seconds, then multiply by four.
03Use the talk test: moderate effort lets you talk, but not sing comfortably.
04

Stay safe

Listen to your body.

Warm up, increase intensity gradually, and cool down after activity. Stop and ask for help if you feel chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or unusual discomfort.

Consistency matters more than chasing a number.

Topic 9

Basic Steps and Techniques for Doing Aerobic Exercises

01

BASIC STEPS AND TECHNIQUES
FOR DOING AEROBIC EXERCISES

ESSENTIAL PHASES OF AN AEROBIC ROUTINE

  • Warm-up: Spend 5 to 10 minutes doing light movements. Like marching in place, shoulder rolls, or gentle step-taps. to increase blood flow and loosen your joints.
  • The Workout: Engage in your chosen continuous activity or dance sequence for 20 to 30 minutes, keeping your breathing steady and controlled.
  • Cool-down: Finish with 5 to 10 minutes of slow walking and static stretches to safely lower your heart rate.
02

Basic Aerobic Dance
& Step Techniques

  • Basic Step: Stand tall with feet hip-width apart. Step forward with your right foot, bring your left foot to meet it, then step back with the right foot and return your left foot to the starting position. Alternate your leading leg with each set.
  • V-Step: Start with feet together. Step your right foot to the front-right diagonal, then step your left foot to the front-left diagonal so your feet form a wide "V" shape. Step back to the center one foot at a time.
  • Step-Touch: Step your right foot to the side, then tap your left foot next to your right foot. Reverse the motion by stepping to the left and tapping your right foot.
  • Grapevine: Step to the side with your right foot, cross your left foot behind or behind-ish past your right foot, step right again, and tap your left foot next to your right.
03

Key Techniques For
Safety And Success

  • Engage your core: Keep your abdominal muscles gently pulled in toward your spine to protect your lower back during standing movements.
  • Land softly: Roll through your foot from heel to toe when stepping or jumping to absorb impact and protect your joints.
  • Keep knees soft: Avoid locking your knees when standing tall or finishing a step.
  • Check your intensity: You should be breathing faster, but you should still be able to talk in short sentences without gasping for air.

Topic 5

Health and Fitness Benefits of Aerobic Exercises

01

HEALTH AND FITNESS BENEFITS
OF AEROBIC EXERCISES

Regular aerobic activity, such as walking, bicycling or swimming, can help you live longer and healthier. Aerobic exercises supports repeated movements in the large muscles in the arms legs and hips. This maximizes the amount of oxygen in the blood increasing blood flow to the muscles, carry away waste products, and allows the body to release endorphins.

No matter your age, weight or athletic ability, aerobic activity is good for you. Aerobic activity has many health benefits. As your body adapts to regular aerobic exercise, you'll get stronger and fitter.

02

Consider the following
10 ways

Consider the following 10 ways that aerobic activity can help you feel better and enjoy life to the fullest.

03

1. Keep excess pounds at bay

Keep excess
pounds at bay

Combined with a healthy diet, aerobic exercise helps you lose weight and keep it off.

04

2. Increase your stamina, fitness and strength

Increase your
stamina, fitness and strength

You may feel tired when you first start regular aerobic exercise. But over the long term, you'll enjoy increased stamina and reduced fatigue.

You can also gain increased heart and lung fitness and bone and muscle strength over time.

05

3. Ward off viral illnesses

Ward off
viral illnesses

Aerobic exercise activates your immune system in a good way. This may leave you less susceptible to viral illnesses, such as colds and the flu.

06

4. Reduce your health risks

Reduce your
health risks

Aerobic exercise reduces the risk of many conditions. These conditions include obesity, heart disease, high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, stroke and certain types of cancer.

Weight-bearing aerobic exercises, such as walking, help lower the risk of osteoporosis.

07

5. Manage chronic conditions

Manage
chronic conditions

Aerobic exercise may help lower blood pressure and control blood sugar. It can reduce pain and improve function in people with arthritis. It can also improve the quality of life and fitness in people who've had cancer. If you have coronary artery disease, aerobic exercise may help you manage your condition.

08

6. Strengthen your heart

Strengthen
your heart

A stronger heart doesn't need to beat as fast. A stronger heart also pumps blood more efficiently, which improves blood flow to all parts of your body.

09

7. Keep your arteries clear

Keep your
arteries clear

Aerobic exercise boosts your high-density lipoprotein (HDL), the "good," cholesterol, and lowers your low-density lipoprotein (LDL), the "bad," cholesterol. This may result in less buildup of plaques in your arteries.

10

8. Boost your mood

Boost
your mood

Aerobic exercise may ease the gloominess of depression, reduce the tension associated with anxiety and promote relaxation. It can improve your mental well-being and your self-esteem. It can also improve your sleep.

11

9. Stay active and independent as you age

Stay active
and independent

Aerobic exercise keeps your muscles strong, which can help you maintain mobility as you get older. Exercise can also lower the risk of falls and injuries from falls in older adults. And it can improve your quality of life.

Aerobic exercise also keeps your mind sharp. Regular physical activity may help protect memory, reasoning, judgment and thinking skills (cognitive function) in older adults. It may also improve cognitive function in children and young adults. It can even help prevent the onset of dementia and improve cognition in people with dementia.

12

10. Live longer

Live longer

Studies show that people who participate in regular aerobic exercise live longer than those who don't exercise regularly. They may also have a lower risk of dying of all causes, such as heart disease and certain cancers.

Aerobic activity has many health benefits. As your body adapts to regular aerobic exercise, you'll get stronger and fitter. Take the first step in becoming healthier and fit, start with small steps and increase the intensity gradually. From there, your halfway through your fitness journey.

Topic 1

Aerobics for Cardiorespiratory Endurance

01

Aerobics for Cardiorespiratory
Endurance

Aerobics for cardiorespiratory endurance means doing continuous, rhythmic exercise to train your heart, lungs, and blood vessels to deliver oxygen to your muscles more efficiently, allowing you to exercise longer without getting tired easily.

♥
02

The Core
Challenge:

  • The Core Challenge: Aerobic exercise continuously challenges your heart, lungs, blood vessels, and working muscles.
MOVE
03

The Immediate
Chain Reaction:

  • Muscles demand an immediate increase in oxygen.
  • The Heart responds by pumping more blood to meet this demand.
  • Breathing automatically becomes faster and deeper.
04

The Foundation of
Endurance:

  • Stamina depends entirely on your body's ability to supply oxygen to muscles continuously.
  • Aerobic exercise maintains this oxygen demand over extended periods.
05

Cardiovascular Response
(The Delivery System)

  • Heart Rate: Increases to pump blood faster.
  • Payload: Delivers vital oxygen and nutrients to active tissues.
  • Result: Regular training strengthens the entire cardiovascular network to support prolonged activity.
06

Respiratory Response
(The Exchange System)

  • The Trigger: Exercise rapidly increases oxygen needs and spikes carbon dioxide production.
  • The Action: Breathing deepens and accelerates to exchange these gases efficiently.
  • Result: Consistent training trains the respiratory system to handle heavy physical demands comfortably.
07

The Process
of Adaptation

  • Beginners: Typically tire quickly during prolonged activity due to unconditioned systems.
  • The Shift: Consistent training prompts the body to transport and use oxygen more efficiently.
  • The Outcome: You can exercise longer, tolerate greater physical demands, and resist fatigue.
08

Managing
Intensity

  • Moderate: Safely increases your heart rate and breathing.
  • Vigorous: Triggers a much stronger cardiovascular and respiratory adaptation.
09

Trainer's Rule
of Thumb

  • Always start with smaller amounts of activity.
  • Gradually build up your duration, frequency, and intensity over time to allow repeated adaptation.

Topic 3

Comparison Between Aerobic and Anaerobic Workouts

01

Comparison Between Aerobic
and Anaerobic Workouts

Aerobic and anaerobic workouts are both forms of physical activity, but they differ in intensity, duration, and how the body produces energy.

⚡
02

Aerobic Workouts

Use oxygen to produce energy and are generally performed at low to moderate intensity for longer periods.

  • Main Goal: Improve cardiovascular endurance and help the heart and lungs work efficiently.
  • Examples: Walking, jogging, swimming, cycling, and dancing.
03

Anaerobic Workouts

Usually short, high-intensity activities that rely mainly on stored energy in the muscles.

  • Main Goal: Developing muscle strength, power, and speed.
  • Examples: Sprinting, heavy resistance exercises, jumping, and other short bursts of intense activity.

Topic 7

Safety Guidelines for Aerobic Exercise Routines

01

SAFETY GUIDELINES FOR
AEROBIC EXERCISE ROUTINES

Before Exercise

  • Prepare properly: Consider your fitness level, previous injuries, and health conditions. Wear comfortable clothing and well-fitting shoes.
  • Warm up: Begin with a proper warm-up to gradually prepare the muscles and body for exercise.
  • Check your environment: Choose a safe area free from obstacles and make sure exercise equipment is suitable and in good condition.
02

SAFETY GUIDELINES FOR
AEROBIC EXERCISE ROUTINES

During Exercise

  • Exercise gradually: Increase the intensity, frequency, and duration according to your ability. Avoid exercising too hard or too fast.
  • Stay hydrated and take breaks: Drink enough water and rest when needed, especially during prolonged exercise or hot and humid weather.
  • Listen to your body: Stop if you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, nausea, unusual fatigue, or significant muscle or joint pain.
03

SAFETY GUIDELINES FOR
AEROBIC EXERCISE ROUTINES

After Exercise

  • Cool down: Gradually reduce the intensity of the exercise and perform gentle stretches.
  • Rehydrate and recover: Drink water after exercising and give your body enough time to recover.
  • Do not ignore pain: If pain or concerning symptoms continue, stop exercising and seek appropriate medical advice.

01

DEFINITION AND NATURE
OF AEROBIC EXERCISE

Aerobic exercise is a physical activity that uses large muscle groups in your body. This type of exercise is usually rhythmic and repetitive. You can adjust the intensity of your workout, which is how hard your body works during this type of exercise.

Nature of

  • Oxygen Utilization: When you practice aerobic exercise, your breathing controls the amount of oxygen that reaches your muscles to help you burn energy and move.

Topic 6

Potential Risks and Precautions

01

POTENTIAL RISKS
AND PRECAUTIONS

  • Muscle strains and sprains: Repetitive, sudden, or improperly performed movements can overstretch muscles, tendons, or ligaments and cause injury.
  • Joint injuries: High-impact movements such as jumping can place repeated stress on the knees, ankles, hips, and lower back, especially when performed frequently or with improper technique.
02

WATCH YOUR
LIMITS

  • Overexertion and excessive fatigue: Exercising beyond one's current fitness level can cause extreme tiredness and may reduce coordination and performance. Beginners are particularly encouraged to increase exercise intensity gradually.
  • Dehydration: Aerobic exercise causes fluid loss through sweating. Prolonged exercise, particularly in hot conditions, can increase the risk of dehydration, which may lead to weakness, dizziness, and reduced exercise performance.
03

ENVIRONMENT
MATTERS

  • Heat-related illness: Performing aerobics in hot or humid environments can increase the risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Symptoms may include heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, dizziness, and shortness of breath.
  • Falls and loss of balance: Fast-paced routines, sudden changes in direction, slippery surfaces, or fatigue can increase the likelihood of stumbling, falling, and sustaining injuries.
04

KNOW WHEN
TO STOP

  • Breathing difficulties: Vigorous aerobic exercise increases the body's demand for oxygen and can cause heavy breathing or shortness of breath, particularly when the intensity is too high for an individual's fitness level.
  • Cardiovascular complications: Aerobic exercise is generally safe and beneficial for most people, but very vigorous exercise can temporarily increase the risk of serious cardiovascular events in susceptible individuals, particularly those with underlying heart conditions.
Exercise safely, increase intensity gradually, and listen to your body.

Topic 8

Recommended Frequency for Aerobic Training

01

RECOMMENDED FREQUENCY
FOR AEROBIC TRAINING

For adults (18–64 years)

  • Moderate-intensity aerobic exercise: 150–300 minutes per week.
  • Example: 30–60 minutes, 5 days a week.
02

VIGOROUS
TRAINING

  • Vigorous-intensity aerobic exercise: 75–150 minutes per week.
  • Example: 25–30 minutes, 3–5 days a week.
  • You can also combine moderate- and vigorous-intensity activities.
03

START SMALL.
BUILD GRADUALLY.

If you're inactive, start with smaller amounts and gradually increase frequency, duration, and intensity.

Consistency and gradual progress help make aerobic training sustainable.
